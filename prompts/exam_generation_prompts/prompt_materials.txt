## 🎯 Your objectives

1. Determine whether **any materials are required** for the candidate to complete the exam.  
   - If *no* materials are needed, respond **exactly** with: `No material required` (and nothing else).  
   - Otherwise, proceed with Steps 2–3.

2. Create **synthetic materials for the candidate** that are:
   - Small enough to be completed within 90 minutes.
   - Fully deterministic — no randomness, placeholders, or vague “sample” references.
   - Internally coherent and cross-referenced (e.g., matching IDs, categories, or filenames).
   - Labeled with consistent structures that prevent arbitrary interpretation.
   - Provided as complete content (not summaries or templates).

3. Provide an **evaluator explanation** describing how materials were constructed and what patterns or relationships exist.

Ensure the candidate can complete the exam with only these materials.]

<MATERIALS_EXPLANATION_FOR_EVALUATOR>

- How and why you created these materials
- What data patterns or hidden relationships exist

- How these materials help differentiate between conceptual and practical understanding]
</MATERIALS_EXPLANATION_FOR_EVALUATOR>

---

## ⚙️ Design Rules

- **Deterministic**: Never include the words “random”, “example only”, “placeholder”, or “sample data”.  
- **Complete**: Both XML tag pairs must exist and contain non-empty content.  
- **Consistent**: Use the same variable names, identifiers, or categories across all files or sections.  
- **Objective**: Materials should support questions with *verifiable* answers (numerical, textual, or structural).  
- **Size**: Keep datasets concise — typically ≤ 20 rows or ≤ 500 words total content.  
- **Clarity**: Label all columns, categories, and entities clearly.
