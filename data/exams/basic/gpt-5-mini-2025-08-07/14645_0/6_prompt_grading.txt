### Exam Purpose
Confirm that the candidate can take a realistic short-project brief and produce a practical, realistic test schedule/strategy that aligns scope, resources, risks and delivery date — i.e., that they can plan test activities so the team can realistically meet the release commitment.

This exam verifies real-world capability to:
- convert project scope and delivery constraints into a concrete, time-boxed test plan and schedule;
- prioritise tests and scope based on risk and constraints;
- estimate effort and allocate limited test resources;
- produce clear entry/exit criteria and contingency decisions to protect delivery quality.

### Core Competencies
The basic exam tests whether the candidate can:
1. Interpret project scope, milestones and constraints.
2. Create a time-based test schedule with milestones and dependencies.
3. Produce simple, justifiable effort estimates and resource assignments.
4. Prioritise features/tests using risk-based reasoning.
5. Define clear entry/exit criteria and contingency (scope-reduction) options when schedule/resource constraints exist.
6. Produce clear, concise documentation (strategy summary + spreadsheet schedule) suitable for hand-off to the team.

### Recommended Structure
Total time: 90 minutes.

Number of tasks: 4 short, sequential, hands-on tasks that mirror a real planning workflow. Each task requires output in a clearly defined JSON structure and uses the spreadsheet (for the schedule) and text editor (for free text summaries). Suggested time allocation:
- Task 1 (10 minutes): Read project brief + state assumptions (deliverable: assumptions list).
- Task 2 (30 minutes): Create a test schedule in spreadsheet form with milestones, dependencies, resource assignment, and a daily/weekly timeline that meets the delivery date (deliverable: schedule data in JSON form).
- Task 3 (30 minutes): Risk-based prioritisation + scope reduction options + effort estimates per feature/test area (deliverable: prioritisation list, estimates, and 2 contingency options).
- Task 4 (20 minutes): Produce a concise test strategy summary including entry/exit criteria, critical open risks and mitigation actions (deliverable: short text summary).

Deliverables (all returned in a single file named test_submission.json):
- assumptions: array of strings

### Practicality — How to make it hands-on
- Give a compact, realistic project brief with clear release date, feature list, limited test team (e.g., 2 testers + part-time automation), environments availability windows, and high-level risks. The brief must include explicit constraints (deadline, resource count, blackout dates).

### Strategic elements (decision points that separate concept from capability)
Include these in the brief so candidates must make trade-offs:
- A fixed release date that leaves very little slack (e.g., 2 weeks for testing) unless scope is reduced.

### Evaluation Insights (what to look for and scoring)
Scoring is itemized so a junior candidate who can do the basics should get >=80%:

Total: 100 points. Passing (>80) for basic exam — target for qualified junior/entry-level.

1. Assumptions (5 pts)
- 5: Clear, relevant assumptions covering at least 3 uncertainties (e.g., environment availability, defect turnaround, stakeholder sign-off).
- 0–4: Missing or irrelevant assumptions.

2. Schedule realism & completeness (30 pts)
- 20 pts: Feasible schedule that respects constraints/dates, shows dependencies, and assigns resources.
- 10 pts: Logical sequencing (test design before execution, environment availability respected) and inclusion of milestones (code freeze, UAT start, release).
- Deductions: unrealistic durations, missing dependencies, resource over-allocation.

3. Estimates (15 pts)
- 10 pts: Reasonable effort estimates per feature and activity aligned with assigned resources.
- 5 pts: Correctly aggregated totals and match schedule durations.
- Deductions for wildly inconsistent numbers (e.g., 2 testers cannot complete 2000 hours in 5 days).

4. Risk-based prioritisation (15 pts)
- 10 pts: Clear priority ranking with concise, logical rationale for top 5 items.
- 5 pts: Correctly identifies highest-risk items and ties prioritisation to release impact.
- Deductions for missing rationale or wrong risk focus.

5. Contingency options (10 pts)
- 7 pts: Two clear, practical options (scope reduction or schedule shift) with impact statements.
- 3 pts: Good rationale and stakeholder considerations.
- Deductions if options are not practical.

6. Entry/Exit criteria & quality gates (10 pts)
- 7 pts: Clear, test-phase appropriate criteria (e.g., pass rate, no open critical defects).
- 3 pts: Practical implementation notes (who signs off).
- Deductions for vague or missing criteria.

7. Strategy summary & communication (10 pts)
- 6 pts: Concise strategy that ties schedule, risk, and priorities together.
- 4 pts: Brief communication plan (who to inform, triggers).
- Deductions for unclear linkage to schedule or missing communications.

Evaluator checks (qualitative):
- Does the schedule meet the release date without impossible assumptions?
- Are dependencies/holidays/environment windows respected?
- Does prioritisation reflect risk to release rather than personal preference?
- Are estimates and resource allocations consistent across schedule and estimate tables?
- Is the strategy document concise and actionable?

Red flags (automatic fail or large deduction):
- Schedule ignores fixed constraints (release date, env blackout).
- No resource assignments or clear over-allocation.
- No contingency plan when schedule is tight.
- Estimates are orders of magnitude unrealistic.

- The materials reproduce the precise compact project brief the candidate needs to plan testing. The brief is deterministic (fixed dates, feature IDs, team availability and environment windows) so candidates must produce concrete schedules and estimates tied to those inputs.

Key patterns and relationships embedded
- Dates form a tight schedule: code freeze 2025-10-20, Test Env A opens 2025-10-21, UAT opens 2025-10-27 and ends 2025-11-01, release 2025-11-03. There is only a small window for system/integration and UAT testing before release — this intentionally forces prioritisation.
- Public holiday 2025-10-23 falls inside Test Environment A's window; candidates must treat that date as non-working.
- Security scan must run in Test Environment A and complete before UAT starts (i.e., on or before 2025-10-26). The candidate must schedule this.
- Test data prep is a shared 2-day task to be scheduled early (after code freeze).
- Automation trade-off: raising automation coverage from 20% to 40% requires significant dedicated automation_setup time from Charlie, who is only 50% available. This creates a realistic resource trade-off for candidates to reason about.
- F2 (Payments) and F5 (Third-party API integration) are high complexity and high risk; F5 has an explicit instability risk callout. Candidates should prioritise these in risk-based ordering.

Traps, likely ambiguities and how to evaluate candidate responses
- Automation interpretation trap: Candidate must explicitly state how they interpret "8 full working days" vs Charlie's 50% availability. Accept either interpretation if the candidate documents it clearly and adjusts schedule and resource calculations consistently. If the candidate assumes 8 calendar days of Charlie time without noting 50% availability, deduct points.
- Security scan scheduling: Many candidates may mistakenly schedule the security scan in UAT; evaluator should check the candidate scheduled the scan in Test Environment A and completed it before UAT start.
- Working day counting: Candidates must count working days and ensure duration_days equals inclusive working days between start_date and end_date, excluding 2025-10-23. Common error: including the public holiday or weekend days. Verify durations match dates.
- Resource over-allocation: Alice and Bob are 100% each; Charlie is 50%. Candidates should not assign Charlie beyond 50% capacity across concurrent tasks unless they document an assumption or schedule automation_setup as an exclusive block (which then makes Charlie unavailable for other tasks). Check that total estimated hours assigned per person per day do not exceed capacity (1 working day = 8 hours; Charlie's capacity per working day = 4 hours).
- Environment windows: Tasks assigned to Test Environment A must lie between 2025-10-21 and 2025-10-30; UAT tasks must lie between 2025-10-27 and 2025-11-01. Evaluate for any schedule that violates these windows.
